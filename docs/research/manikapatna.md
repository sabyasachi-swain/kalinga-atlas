## Exact references used

### `tripati-2021-current-science`

Sila Tripati, "Indo-Arabian stone anchor of Manikapatna, Odisha, east coast of India: an indicator
of maritime contacts of Odisha", *Current Science* 120(7), 10 April 2021, pp. 1211–1216.

- p. 1211: "Importance of Manikapatna as a port was unknown before 1989. The Odishan Institute of
  Maritime and Southeast Asian Studies (OIMSEAS), Bhubaneswar, undertook excavations between 1989
  and 1993".
- p. 1211: "Manikapatna, which lies on the bank of Chilika Lake, in Brahmagiri taluk of Puri
  district, Odisha, served as a port from the early historical period to the medieval period."
- p. 1212: "the findings of Manikapatna were broadly divided into two periods, namely, from 2nd
  century BCE to 5th–6th century CE and from 9th to 19th century CE. The excavation findings
  included Khorasthi inscription, Knobbed ware, Rouletted ware, local pottery, beads, stone and
  terracotta artefacts and figurines. Coins of the Puri-Kushan period (1st century CE), Rajaraja
  Chola (985–1016 CE), Sahassamalla of Polonnaruva period, Sri Lanka and Jiaqing Emperor of Qing
  Dynasty, China (18th century CE) as well as Chinese porcelain (1368–1644 CE) … belonging to Yuan
  and Ming dynasties are also recorded".

### `patra-patra-ohrj-maritime-archaeology`

Sushanta Ku. Patra & Dr. Benudhar Patra, "Archaeology and the Maritime History of Ancient Orissa",

- p. 111: "The discovery of Chinese celadone ware, white porcelain, blue, white and brown glazed

  pottery, Ceylonese coins, Siamese pottery, Indonesian terracotta, egg white Arabian pottery
  moulded ware, stamped ware, Decorated were, kaolin ware etc. at Manikpatna testify to its trade
  link with far off countries."
- p. 111: "Manikapatna recently has been identified with the Che-li-ta-lo port of Hiuen Tsang".
- p. 111: Ceylonese coin with the legend "Srimad Sahassamalla" (c. 11th–12th c. CE).
- pp. 111–112: two-line Kharoshthi potsherd read by B. N. Mukherjee as "Dasatradeva" and "Khida",
  2nd century CE — "the only instance of a Kharosthi inscription in the whole of Kalinga or even in
  eastern and southeastern India."

### `patnaik-2014-odisha-review`

Sunil Kumar Patnaik, "Puri: A Study of Early Trade and Pilgrim Routes", *Odisha Review*, June 2014,
pp. 103–109; Manikapatna at pp. 104–105.

- p. 104: "The Archaeological excavation at Manikapatna, about 45 kms towards south of Puri
  provides a running chronology of historical habitation being a port or trade centre from 1st
  century A.D. to 18th century A.D."
- p. 104: "The limited excavation revealed (1989-90, 1992-93) a cultural deposit of two periods";
  period I dated 2nd century BCE to 5th–6th century CE, ending in a hiatus; period II 9th century
  to the early part of the 19th century CE.
- p. 104: series of mounds, with the Bhabakundaleswar temple of the 13th century CE on the highest,
  and a Muslim tomb datable to 1885 CE to its east. Abul Fazl described Manikapatna as a sea port
  where taxes were collected.
- p. 105: finds include "two triangular copper coins of Shaha Alam, two east India copper coins, a
  British Medal, Mughal coins, a Chinese coin", rouletted ware, knobbed ware, Chinese celadon and
  porcelain, "a shred with Khorosti script and a Puri-Kushana coin".

## Corrections to the task brief

- **Not an ASI excavation.** Every source consulted attributes the 1989–93 excavation to the
  **Odishan Institute of Maritime and Southeast Asian Studies (OIMSEAS), Bhubaneswar**, a
  Government of Odisha body — not to the ASI, and not to the Odisha State Department of
  Archaeology. A second campaign was run by **Deccan College, Pune, in 2010** (Tripati et al. 2022).
  By contrast the nearby port of Khalkatapatna *was* an ASI excavation (1987), per *Odisha Review*
  2014 p. 105 — the two are easy to conflate.
- The registry source `asi-annual-reports` is therefore **not** cited for this entry.

## Coordinates — how they were derived, and why they are flagged

No source I read printed a latitude or longitude for Manikapatna. `coordinates` are approximate,
derived from two published statements plus a modern map, and this is stated in `caveats`:

- "on the bank of Chilika Lake, in Brahmagiri taluk of Puri district" (Tripati 2021, p. 1211)
- "about 45 kms towards south of Puri" (Patnaik 2014, p. 104)

`coordinate_source` is set to `tripati-2021-current-science`, the source of the locational
description, not of a measured coordinate. **A reviewer should replace 19.68 / 85.47 with a
gazetteer or site-plan figure before this entry is promoted.**

Beware: web results offering "GPS coordinates" of 20.58–20.61 N / 86.57–86.65 E refer to a
*different* Manikapatna in Kendrapara district. Those values are wrong for this site and were
rejected.

`mauryan` (−300 to −185) was **excluded**: Period I begins "2nd century BCE", which overlaps the

**Gap found in `periods.json`:** nothing covers c. 1 CE – 300 CE, which is precisely where the
Puri-Kushan coin (1st c. CE) and the Kharoshthi potsherd (2nd c. CE) sit — the finds that carry
most of the site's Roman-network significance. There is a second gap at 600–700 CE. This is a data
question for a human, not something to fix by stretching an existing period.
