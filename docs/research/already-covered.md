# Kalinga Atlas: what we already cover

*Updated 18 September 2026. Hand this to anyone researching for the atlas.*

The atlas maps Kalinga (Odisha) trade from about 300 BCE to 1900 CE. Everything
below is **already in it, with sources**. Please do not research these topics
again.

**One exception.** A covered topic is still worth sending back if you bring one
of these:

- a **new, independent source** we do not already hold (see section 6);
- a **correction** to what we say;
- a **firm date** where we only have a rough one.

Entries marked *(one source)* are the ones where a second independent source
helps most.

---

## 3. Routes already mapped

- **Coast of Odisha:** Palur–Sonapur–Barua–Kalingapatnam; Manikapatna–Puri; Manikapatna–Khalkatapatna; Manikapatna–Tamralipti; Pipli–Balasore; Balugaon–Puri and Ganjam–Puri across Chilika.
- **Overseas:** Chilika coast–Sri Lanka; Palur–Bali; Chilika coast–Arikamedu; Tamralipti–Mantai; Kedah–Tamralipti; Palembang–Kedah; Guangzhou–Palembang (the Yijing voyage); Gopalpur–Rangoon.
- **Overland:** Dhauli–Jaugada; Jaugada–Palur; Sisupalgarh–Manikapatna; Udayagiri–Pithunda; the Orissa Trunk Road (Puri–Balasore).
- **River and canal:** Cuttack–Sambalpur (Mahanadi); Cuttack–Chandbali and Cuttack–False Point canals; Dhamra–Chandbali; Chandbali–Calcutta and Balasore–Calcutta steamers.

## 4. Goods already covered

**Before 1500:** ivory *(one source)*, elephants, cotton cloth, cowrie shells,
Chinese ceramics, amphora jars, Roman coins.

**1500–1900:** salt, rice, diamonds, sal timber, iron, tasar silk, lac and
beeswax, coconuts, coral, dried fish, Bengal silk, tobacco, turmeric, oilseeds,
ghee, molasses, brass utensils, earthen pots, catechu, stone plates.

## 5. Inscriptions and single facts

- **Inscriptions:** Hathigumpha (Jayaswal & Banerji and Sircar editions, both
  read); Ashoka's Separate Edicts at Dhauli and Jaugada; Bodhisiri's inscription
  at Nagarjunakonda (names Tosali; the "Palura" restoration is only Vogel's
  suggestion); the Manikapatna Kharoshthi potsherd (Mukherjee's reading). *A
  reported re-reading by Stefan Baums (2020) is wanted; please send the exact
  citation if you find it.*
- **Facts:** Ashoka's inspectors from Ujjain and Taxila; Kharavela's "China
  clothes"; Pithunda ploughed with donkeys; Indian pots in Bali; Brahmeswar
  temple ship with an elephant; cowries as money; Maldive boats at Balasore;
  salt boats up the Mahanadi; Bali Jatra and Boita Bandana (tradition only).
- **Kharavela had a navy:** already checked. The inscription does not say so,
  and the atlas does not claim it.

## 7. Already checked and rejected

Please do not send these back unless you have a **primary source** we have not
seen:

- Islamic glazed ware at Manikapatna dated 8th–14th century: no source gives
  that date.
- Tang or Song dynasty ceramics at Manikapatna: none found in the excavation
  papers.
- A Chilika landing at Chandrabhaga: Chandrabhaga is the Konark beach, not on
  the lake.
- Odia boat names and boat-building words (*boita* etc.): only blogs and tourism
  sites found. A scholarly source would be welcome.
- The Nagarjunakonda inscription "names Palur as a port": the name is damaged
  on the stone.
- Tamralipti "was part of Odisha": a contested political claim, not adopted.
- The Konark "giraffe" as proof of African trade, and the claim that Sisupalgarh
  had "twice the people of Athens": popular claims with no usable source.

## 8. What makes a source usable

- A real publication with an author, a year and a page number. A DOI or a
  publisher link is best.
- No Wikipedia, blogs, tourism sites, wisdomlib-style compilations or AI
  summaries. Those are fine as leads, never as sources.
- One source cannot make a claim "certain". Say how sure the source itself is.
- Keep archaeological finds, scholars' interpretations and traditions apart.
