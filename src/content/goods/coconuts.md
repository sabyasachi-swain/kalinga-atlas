A British officer named Stirling wrote about ships from the Maldive Islands. They sailed to Balasore and Dhamra every year. They carried coconuts, cowrie shells, coral and dried fish. Traders swapped these for rice and clay pots. [Stirling, 1825, p. 194]

An 1872 book tells a different story. River towns carried coconuts up the Mahanadi river to Sambalpur. Salt, spices and brass pots went on the same boats. [Hunter, 1872, vol. II Appendix III, p. 74]

## For scholars

Stirling names the Maldive Islands as the source of the coconuts at Balasore and Dhamra. Hunter's coconuts move inland, to Sambalpur. He does not say where they first came from. They might be coconuts grown on the Odisha coast itself, not imported ones. Read together, the two sources show that coconuts were a real trade good here. They do not prove one single import route. [Stirling, 1825, p. 194; Hunter, 1872, vol. II Appendix III, p. 74]
