title: Diamonds
---

Hirakud is an island in the Mahanadi river. Its name means "diamond island." A report says that before British rule, searchers called Jharias sifted its sands for diamonds. They were paid with grants of land [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.12].

The same report calls these stones "of very poor quality." It says the British government later leased the right to dig them. The price was just two hundred rupees a year. The lease was later given up [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.12].

A Greek writer named Ptolemy wrote about an inland town. He called it Sambalaka. He also named a river Adamas, the Greek word for diamond. Some later notes say Sambalaka may be Sambalpur. Historians think this may be an old link to these diamonds. But one scholar argued against it [McCrindle, 1927, p.169].

## For scholars
Only the colonial gazetteer records real diamond digging. Even it calls the stones poor quality. The old link rests on two guesses. One guess says Sambalaka is Sambalpur. The other says the river Adamas is the Mahanadi. A scholar named V. Ball rejected part of this idea in 1883. He named a different river instead. That river has no diamonds at all. Also, Sambalpur was not part of Orissa when this gazetteer was written.
