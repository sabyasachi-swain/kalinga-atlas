Small, shiny sea shells called cowries worked as everyday money in Odisha. A Chinese monk visited the Kongoda coast in the 600s CE. He wrote that people there paid with cowrie shells and pearls [Xuanzang, 7th century CE, p.207].

Fresh cowries kept arriving by sea for centuries. [NEEDS VERIFICATION] <!-- fact-check: the entry caveat says nothing bridges the 600s and 1825. "For centuries" claims a run of trade no source shows. They also brought coconuts, coral and dried fish [Stirling, 1825, p.194].

One number tells the story well. In 1804, one silver rupee bought 5,120 cowries. By the 1870s, the same rupee bought only 3,584. ## For scholars
The Chinese monk does not say where his cowries came from. So the import claim rests on the 1825 report alone. Nothing found so far bridges the gap between the 600s and 1825. So those centuries are left untagged here.
