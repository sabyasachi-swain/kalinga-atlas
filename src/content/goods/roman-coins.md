Roman gold coins have been found in the Mayurbhanj hills. The spot is called Bamanghati. Historians think they probably came overland through the port of Tamralipti. A lot of rouletted ware, a stamped pottery, came the same way. [Patra, n.d., p. 110]

Roman coins have also turned up at Kalingapatnam. This old port sits at the mouth of the Vamsadhara river. Digs there, in 1928 and again in 1977-79, found pottery, brick walls and glazed tiles. They dated the settlement back to 300 BCE. [Patra, 2014, pp. 122-123]

## For scholars

Both reports come from the same writer, Benudhar Patra. So they count as one source, not two. That keeps the tier at Probable. The Mayurbhanj coins are an Odisha find. But scholars argue they came overland from the Bengal port of Tamralipti. They did not come by sea into Kalinga. Neither source names the coins' Roman ruler or date. [Patra, n.d., p. 110; Patra, 2014, pp. 122-123]
