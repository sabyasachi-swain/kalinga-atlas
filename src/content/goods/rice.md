Rice was grown across Odisha. A considerable amount of it left by sea from several small ports along the coast. It was carried away to Calcutta [Stirling, 1825, p.194].

Here is one striking detail. Ships from the Maldive Islands sailed all the way to Balasore and Dhamra each year. They came to fill their holds with rice and clay pots [Stirling, 1825, p.194].

A report on Puri district names one busy spot. At Machhagaon, near the mouth of the Devi river, "a considerable export rice trade" once took place [Hunter, 1872, p.13].

Further inland, in Sambalpur district, a gazetteer from around 1903 calls rice "the staple export." It went mainly to Calcutta over land, with some on to Bombay and Berar. This was inland trade, not sea trade [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.13].

## For scholars
Every source used here comes from the colonial period. Rice was surely grown and eaten here much earlier. But no dated source shows it as a traded good before 1825. So no earlier period is claimed [Stirling, 1825, p.194; Hunter, 1872, p.13].
