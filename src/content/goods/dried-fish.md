A British officer named Stirling wrote about ships from the Maldive Islands. They sailed to Balasore and Dhamra every year, in the 1820s. They carried a small load of dried fish, along with cowrie shells, coconuts and coral. [Stirling, 1825, p. 194]

About a century later, fish from Chilika lake moved a different way. A 1929 gazetteer says fish and grain left the lake town of Balugaon by train. The government's own fishery officer had just moved there from Puri. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Mansfield, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Puri (1929), pp. 204-205]

## For scholars

These two records describe different things, a century apart. Stirling's dried fish is a small import, carried by Maldive ships in the 1820s. The 1929 gazetteer's fish is a lake catch, sent out by rail, with no stated overseas destination. They are combined here because both are verified records of fish moving as cargo through this coast. They do not describe the same trade route. [Stirling, 1825, p. 194; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Mansfield, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Puri (1929), pp. 204-205]
