title: Cotton cloth and muslin
---

Kautilya's old handbook on government named the seven best cotton-weaving lands in India. Kalinga made that list [Kautilya, n.d., p.84].

Almost two thousand years later, English traders still wanted cloth from this coast. A report from the 1600s says company buyers bought "the fine muslins of Cattack" at the lowest price they could get [Hunter, 1872, p.40].

By the 1820s, cloth moved both ways. A British survey names cloth as the region's top taxed export. It also names cloth as a good bought in from nearby Bengal [Stirling, 1825, p.194].

## For scholars
Nothing links the ancient cotton to the later muslin trade. The two claims rest on separate sources. Almost 1,800 years lie silent between them. The famous muslins in the old Greek sailing guide, the Periplus, belong to Masalia. Masalia is usually placed near Machilipatnam in Andhra, not in Kalinga.
