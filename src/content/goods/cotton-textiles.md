title: Cotton cloth and muslin
---

Kautilya's old handbook on government named the seven best cotton-weaving lands in India. Kalinga made that list [Kautilya, n.d., p.84].

English traders still wanted cloth from this coast much later. A report from 1872 describes buyers in the 1680s who bought "the fine muslins of Cattack" at the lowest price they could get [Hunter, 1872, p.40].

By the 1820s, cloth moved both ways. A British survey lists piece goods first among the taxed exports. It also names cloth as a good bought in from nearby Bengal [Stirling, 1825, p.194].

## For scholars
Nothing links the ancient cotton to the later muslin trade. The two claims rest on separate sources, and almost 1,800 years lie silent between them [Kautilya, n.d., p.84; Hunter, 1872, p.40].
