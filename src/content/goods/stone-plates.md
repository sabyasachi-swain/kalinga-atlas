An 1825 British survey lists stone plates among the goods taxed on leaving Cuttack. They travelled out alongside sal timber and other forest goods. The survey does not say what stone they were carved from. [Stirling, 1825, p. 194]

## For scholars

Only one source documents this, so the tier stops at Probable. Stirling names "stone plates" but not the stone type, where it was quarried, or how the plates were used. Do not assume soapstone, or any one craft tradition, without another source. [Stirling, 1825, p. 194]
