entity_id: tasar-silk
title: Tasar silk
---

Wild silkworms live in the hill jungles. They spin a coarse, golden silk called tasar. A report names a whole community, the Kostas, who made their living trading it [Hunter, 1872, p.75].

Weavers in Sambalpur turned the silk into cloth. A gazetteer says the cocoons themselves came from Chota Nagpur. Only "a little" of the finished cloth reached Ganjam near the coast. Most of it stayed and sold close to home [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.13].

## For scholars
Both sources here come from the late colonial period. Nothing found carries tasar's trade back further than that. The cocoons were themselves brought in from elsewhere. Only a small amount of cloth reached the coast. So this looks like a local trade, not a proven sea export. One earlier report even shows silk moving the other way. It says silk was imported into Cuttack from Bengal.
