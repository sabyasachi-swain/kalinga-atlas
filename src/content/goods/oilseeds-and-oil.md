River boats carried oil-seeds and oil down the Mahanadi river. They went from Sambalpur to the Odisha coast. [Hunter, 1872, vol. II Appendix III, p. 74]

An 1872 book lists oil-seeds too. It names them as everyday goods. Inland areas called the Tributary States traded them. Historians think oil-seeds were a normal trade good there. [Hunter, 1872, vol. II, p. 102]

## For scholars

Both references come from the same book, by Hunter. So only one source supports this entry. That keeps the tier at Probable. Neither passage says the oil-seeds left India. Both describe river and inland trade only. [Hunter, 1872, vol. II Appendix III, p. 74; Hunter, 1872, vol. II, p. 102]
