title: Lac and beeswax
---

Tiny insects living on forest trees make a sticky red resin. It is called lac. Forest people gathered it, along with wax from wild bees. They carried both out of the hills [Hunter, 1872, p.75].

An 1825 tax list names beeswax and lac among the goods taxed as they left the district [Stirling, 1825, p.194].

Whole communities lived by this work. A report names one forest people, the Kols, as those "who sell resin, lac, wax" for a living [Hunter, 1872, p.75].

## For scholars
Neither source says lac or wax was shipped overseas. Stirling's list records tax paid on leaving the district. Hunter's report describes inland trade, not sea trade [Stirling, 1825, p.194; Hunter, 1872, p.75]. This good should not be called a sea export. That would need a customs record or a trading company's own books.
