title: Hathigumpha Inscription of Kharavela
---

Hathigumpha means "Elephant Cave." It is a cave cut into a hill near Bhubaneswar, in Odisha. Long lines are carved on its wall. They tell the story of a king named Kharavela [Sircar, 1965, p.213].

The words tell of things brought back from far away. Kharavela had elephants, jewels, and pearls brought here from a king of the Pandyas, to the south, after breaking a league against him [Jayaswal, 1930, p.88]. Kharavela also gave a kind of cloth called "China clothes" to monks - a fabric name, not a sign that the cloth came from China [Jayaswal, 1930, p.88].

Scholars care about this cave because it holds Kharavela's own words. But the rock has big cracks. Some words are hard to read. Two scholars have read some lines in very different ways, so no one line is fully settled [Sircar, 1965, p.217].

## For scholars

Even the writing's age is debated. One study says it was cut in the early 100s BCE. A later study says it was cut near the end of the 1st century BCE, over 150 years after that first date [Sircar, 1965, p.213].

One damaged line matters most for trade. Early readers thought it named a port town. A later scholar read different letters there. He said the port-town reading cannot be trusted. Until the rock is read again, neither guess should be called the truth [Sircar, 1965, p.217].
