title: The Kharoshthi Potsherd from Manikapatna
---

Manikapatna is an old port by Chilika lake, in Odisha. Workers digging there found a broken piece of pottery. Two lines were scratched onto it. The letters are Kharoshthi, a script from far-off northwest India [Patra, n.d., p.112].

A scholar read the scratches as two words: "Dasatradeva" and "Khida." He thought the pot was made about 1,900 years ago. Historians say this may be the only Kharoshthi writing ever found on the whole coast of eastern India [Patra, n.d., p.112].

This pot is a clue. Someone who knew a script from northwest India once stood at this port. But it does not prove the pot itself traveled far. Someone could have scratched the words right there [Patra, n.d., p.112].

## For scholars

The reading "Dasatradeva" and "Khida" comes from one scholar's study. It was reported inside another writer's article. No one on this project has seen the first report that named it, so the words are recorded here as reported, not checked firsthand [Patra, n.d., p.118].

The pot's exact language is not known. Only its script, Kharoshthi, is known. Script and language are not the same thing, so this clue should not be stretched too far [Patra, n.d., p.112].
