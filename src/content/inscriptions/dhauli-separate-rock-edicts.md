title: Ashoka's Separate Rock Edicts at Dhauli
---

Long ago, King Ashoka had two messages cut into a rock at Dhauli. Dhauli is a hill near Bhubaneswar. The messages are for his officers in a town called Tosali. One message says to check on people every five years. Officers also came from Ujjayini and Takshasila, far-off cities. The messages say nothing about ships, goods, or traders [Hultzsch, 1925, p.97].

A different scholar studied the same writing on his own. [NEEDS VERIFICATION] <!-- fact-check: Vogel edited the Nagarjunakonda inscription, not the Dhauli edicts. He used Dhauli to place Tosali. These messages are some of the oldest writing found in Odisha. But no dig has found exactly where Tosali stood. Some historians guess it was a fort called Sisupalgarh. That is still just a guess [Patra, n.d., p.110].

## For scholars

No dig has ever proven where Tosali stood. Sisupalgarh is a huge fort nearby, from about the same time. So some historians guess it was Tosali. That guess rests on size and date alone. No writing found there names the town [Patra, n.d., p.110].

People often call these two edicts "edict one" and "edict two." Long ago, a surveyor said the two labels are swapped. Most books still use the old names, just to avoid confusion [Hultzsch, 1925, pp.xiii-xiv].
