title: Ashoka's Separate Rock Edicts at Dhauli
---

Long ago, King Ashoka had two messages carved into a rock at Dhauli, a hill near Bhubaneswar. The messages are for his officers in a town called Tosali. One message tells an officer to check on people every five years. It says officers from far-off cities, Ujjayini and Takshasila, made the same kind of trips. The messages say nothing about ships, goods, or merchants [Separate Rock Edicts of Ashoka at Dhauli and Jaugada, c. 3rd century BCE, p.97].

A different scholar studied the same writing on his own. He agreed that Tosali really was a Mauryan town on this coast [Epigraphia Indica, n.d., Vol. XX, p.8].

Scholars care about these messages because they are some of the oldest writing found in Odisha. But no dig has found exactly where Tosali stood. Some historians guess it was a nearby fort called Sisupalgarh. That is still just a guess [Patra, n.d., p.110].

## For scholars

No dig has ever proven exactly where Tosali stood. Sisupalgarh is a huge fort nearby, from about the same time. So some historians guess it was Tosali. But that is a guess based on size and date. No writing found there names the town [Patra, n.d., p.110].

People often call these two edicts "edict one" and "edict two." Long ago, a surveyor argued that the two labels are mixed up. Most books still use the old names, just to avoid confusion [Separate Rock Edicts of Ashoka at Dhauli and Jaugada, c. 3rd century BCE, p.14].
