title: Ashoka's Separate Rock Edicts at Jaugada
---

The same two royal messages that Ashoka carved at Dhauli were also cut into a rock at Jaugada. Jaugada is inside an old fort by the Rishikulya river, in Ganjam district, Odisha. Here, the messages are for officers in a town called Samapa [Separate Rock Edicts of Ashoka at Dhauli and Jaugada, c. 3rd century BCE, p.117].

Historians agree this fixes a second Mauryan town on the Odisha coast, close to the sea. Diggers at Jaugada also found old coins, pottery, and an earthen wall [Patra, n.d., p.109].

One historian thinks the messages were placed here because of a nearby port called Palur. That is just his own idea. The messages themselves say nothing about ports, ships, or goods [Patra, n.d., p.113].

## For scholars

Much of the Jaugada rock has crumbled away. About half of the first message is lost. About a third of the second message is lost too. Those missing words can never be read again [Separate Rock Edicts of Ashoka at Dhauli and Jaugada, c. 3rd century BCE, p.14].

The port-of-Palur idea is a guess. It is not something written in the stone. Readers should not treat it as settled fact [Patra, n.d., p.113].
