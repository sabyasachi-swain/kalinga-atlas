Ujjayini was one of four great capitals in Ashoka's empire. It stood far to the west of Kalinga. A prince ruled there as governor. Other governors sat at Taxila, in the northwest, and at Tosali, in Kalinga. [Kulke, 1998, 3rd edn (Routledge, 1998), p. 67]

Ashoka's own rock-carved orders name Ujjayini too. They say the governor sent out an officer every three years. This officer checked that Ashoka's laws were followed. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, CII I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause AA, p. 97; identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]

## For scholars

Ujjayini is not a Kalinga site. It appears here for one reason. Ashoka's own edicts name it as the western capital. They link it to Kalinga through this touring-officer rule. Scholars have long matched Ujjayini to the modern city of Ujjain. No other guess was found. Neither source prints a coordinate. The position given is modern Ujjain, read from a map. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, CII I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause AA, p. 97; identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]
