Near Bhubaneswar stands a huge square fort called Sisupalgarh. Its earth wall runs over a mile long on each side. Archaeologists found pottery styles that travelled far. One kind, rouletted ware, has a stamped pattern. It turns up from Tamil Nadu all the way to Bali. Diggers also found small clay seals copied from Roman coin pictures [Patra, n.d., p.110].

In 2008, a team from Pune and California dug into the north wall. They found eighteen huge stone pillars, never seen before. They may be part of a large public building. Its full shape is still unclear [Mohanty, 2009, pp.50-52].

## For scholars
Some scholars link Sisupalgarh to Tosali, the capital Ashoka set up here. But this is a proposal, not a proven fact. The Dhauli edicts are the usual anchor for that name instead. The clay seals with Roman coin designs are local copies. They are not Roman objects themselves. They show contact with a wide trading world, not a Roman visit.
