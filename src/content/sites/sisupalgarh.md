title: Sisupalgarh
---

Near Bhubaneswar stands a huge walled city called Sisupalgarh. It has an earth wall and moat around more than a square kilometre of land. People lived here from about the 3rd century BCE to the mid-4th century CE [Patra, n.d., p.110; Mohanty, 2009, pp.47-49].

Archaeologists found pottery styles that travelled far. One kind, rouletted ware, has a stamped pattern. It turns up from Tamil Nadu all the way to Bali. Diggers also found small clay seals copied from Roman coin pictures [Patra, n.d., p.110].

In 2008, a team from Pune and California dug into the north wall. They found eighteen huge stone pillars, never seen before. They may be part of a large public building. Its full shape is still unclear [Mohanty, 2009, pp.50-52].

Scholars think this city was not the end of the story. A paper's summary says a new settlement grew up two kilometres away, starting around the 6th century CE, after Sisupalgarh itself was left behind [Smith, 2025, abstract].

## For scholars
Some scholars link Sisupalgarh to Tosali, the capital Ashoka set up here. But this is a proposal, not a proven fact. The Dhauli edicts are the usual anchor for that name instead. The clay seals with Roman coin designs are local copies. They are not Roman objects themselves. They show contact with a wide trading world, not a Roman visit. The first dig report, from 1949, was not read directly for this entry [Patra, n.d., p.110; Mohanty, 2009, pp.47-48].
