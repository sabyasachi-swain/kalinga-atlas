title: Salihundam
---

Salihundam is a hill covered in old Buddhist buildings. It sits beside the Vamsadhara river in Andhra Pradesh. Monks lived here for about a thousand years. That was from around 200 BCE to the 700s or 800s CE [Subrahmanyam, 1964, pp.2-3, 9].

Diggers found five separate monasteries on the hill. Each one had its own kitchen and dining hall. This shows many monks lived and ate together here, for a long time [Subrahmanyam, 1964, pp.29-30].

Archaeologists also found rouletted ware. This is a fine pottery stamped with tiny rolled lines. It looks like ware found at Arikamedu and at Odisha's own old ports. Historians agree this pottery ties Salihundam to a wide trading network around the Bay of Bengal [Subrahmanyam, 1964, pp.8-9; Patra, n.d., p.110].

Diggers also found two shells. Both were carved with a name close to "Salipataka" or "Salipedaka." This may once have been an old name for the site itself [Subrahmanyam, 1964, p.4].

## For scholars

The dot marking Salihundam on this atlas's map is approximate. The excavation report's own printed coordinates (18°13' N, 84°15' E) fall in the sea. So the position here is worked out a different way. It follows the report's own words: on the Vamsadhara, about four miles from Kalingapatnam. That spot is then matched against a modern coastline map. The report also mentions small clay seals copying Roman style. It reports Roman coins found near Kalingapatnam too. But it names no emperor. The one sentence on these coins calls them only "issues of later emperors." A popular claim that one coin belonged to the emperor Tiberius does not appear anywhere in this source [Subrahmanyam, 1964, p.iv, p.3, p.14].
