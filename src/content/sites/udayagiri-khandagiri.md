title: Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves
---

Two rocky hills near Bhubaneswar are full of caves. A colonial report corrects an old mistake. The caves were cut for Jain monks, not Buddhist ones, as people often thought [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.239].

One cave carries a long inscription. It tells the story of King Kharavela's wars and building work. In it, he boasts of destroying a coastal market town called Pithumda. He also brought pearls home from a king far to the south [Jayaswal, 1930, p.88].

Carvings inside the caves show patterns like honeysuckle and acanthus leaves. These styles first grew popular far to the west. Scholars say this shows ideas travelled the same routes as goods [Patra, n.d., p.115].

## For scholars
The date of King Kharavela's inscription is disputed. Some scholars place him near 200 BCE. Others place him near 1 BCE. Several lines of the inscription have more than one accepted reading, including the line about Pithumda. Linking Pithumda to a town named by the Greek writer Ptolemy is a scholarly guess. It is not a settled fact.
