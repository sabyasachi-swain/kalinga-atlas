Golbai Sasan is a village mound near Chilika lake. People lived here for thousands of years. Their story starts in the Stone Age, long before Kalinga's kings and ports [Patra, n.d., p.108].

Diggers found stone tools called adzes, used for shaping wood. They also found a bone fish hook and a harpoon made of antler. [NEEDS VERIFICATION] <!-- fact-check: the entry finds give a copper fish hook and a bone harpoon. The two materials are swapped here. --> Scholars think the many wood tools may mean people built boats here. But no actual boat has ever turned up [Patra, n.d., p.109].

Diggers also found burnt grains of rice, pulses and millet. Radiocarbon dating shows farming here goes back thousands of years [Kingwell-Banham, 2018, pp.5-9].

## For scholars
The site's main period of life falls before this atlas's earliest era. So only its later, Iron Age layer gets a date here. The idea that people built boats here comes from woodworking tools, not from any real boat. One coordinate in an early report was a misprint, pointing out at sea. This entry's location is fixed using a modern map instead.
