Takshashila was Ashoka's capital in the far northwest. It sat near modern Pakistan. A prince governed there too. Other governors sat at Ujjain, in the west, and Tosali, in Kalinga. [Kulke, 1998, 3rd edn (Routledge, 1998), p. 67]

Ashoka's own rock-carved orders name Takshashila as well. An officer was sent out from there too. This checked that his laws were followed, just as at Ujjayini. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, CII I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause BB, p. 97; identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]

## For scholars

Takshashila is not a Kalinga site. It appears here for one reason. Ashoka's own edicts name it as the northwestern capital. They link it to Kalinga through this touring-officer rule. A scholar named Cunningham matched it to Shahdheri, near modern Taxila. That is the guess used here. A later city dug up by John Marshall was not read in this session. Neither source prints a coordinate. The position given is the modern Taxila site, read from a map. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, CII I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause BB, p. 97; identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]
