Balasore was Odisha's busiest sea port before the railway came. A colonial report says it was once "the only port" Odisha had. This was true at the start of the 1800s. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.144]

Ships from Balasore carried rice north to Calcutta. Some ships sailed on to Bombay. Some sailed all the way to Burma. A British officer wrote about this trade in the 1820s. He saw rice moving up the coast from small ports. Much of it went to Calcutta. [Stirling, 1825, p.194]

Fine cotton cloth called muslin left from here too. Long ago, English traders bought this cloth at Balasore. They paid very low prices. Then they sent the cloth home by ship. [Hunter, 1872, p.40]

## For scholars

Hunter's muslin story is old. It tells of the 1600s and 1700s. Back then, English trading posts bought cloth at Balasore. It shows us the cargo, not this later Calcutta run. Only the British period is used here. Both sources that name Calcutta as the goal come from the 1800s. [Hunter, 1872, p.40; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.144]
