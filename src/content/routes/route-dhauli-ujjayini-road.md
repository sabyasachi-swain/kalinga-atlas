Ashoka carved a rule at Dhauli. It said an officer had to come all the way from Ujjayini, a city far to the west. This inspector's first trip came after five years. After that, one had to arrive at least every three years. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause AA, p. 97; identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]

Scholars agree Ujjayini and Kalinga were both run by the same Mauryan government. A prince governed in the west, at Ujjain. Another governed in the east, at Tosali in Kalinga. [Kulke, 1998, 3rd edn (Routledge, 1998), p. 67]

## For scholars

This edict is strong evidence for a real route. It orders officials to travel this exact circuit, on a schedule, not just naming two related towns. Even so, no source describes the road itself, its course or its stations. The line shown, about 1,400 km long, is schematic. The Dhauli copy of this rule is addressed to officials at Tosali. The same rule appears on a second copy at Jaugada, addressed to officials at Samapa instead. That is why this atlas records a separate Jaugada-to-Ujjayini route too. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause AA, p. 97; identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]
