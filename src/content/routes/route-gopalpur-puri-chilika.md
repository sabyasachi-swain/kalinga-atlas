title: Ganjam to Puri through Chilika
scholar: false
---

Boats from the Ganjam coast went north to Puri. They used a canal, then crossed Chilika lake. A colonial report says Ganjam traders used this canal and lake all year round. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.196; Patnaik, 2014, p.106]

These boats brought bamboo north. They carried spare rice back south. The rice came from villages near the lake. [Patnaik, 2014, p.106]

One historian says that pilgrims went to Puri in 1810. Some crossed Chilika by boat. Most walked instead, along the strip of land beside the lake. [Patnaik, 2014, p.108]

## For scholars

Old Kushana coins found near the lake hint at a much older trade route. So does a mention of Chilika in the Brahmanda Purana. But the boat trips named in these sources are from British times. So only that later time is shown here. Gopalpur stands in for the wider Ganjam coast. The report names Ganjam traders and the Ganjam canal, not Gopalpur itself. [Patnaik, 2014, p.106; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.196]
