A great road was cut north from Puri. It ran through Cuttack, all the way to Balasore. Colonial engineers built it between 1812 and 1820. People also called it the Jagannath Road. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.146; Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

Crowds of pilgrims walked this road. They went to see the great temple of Jagannath, at Puri. A historian traces its path. It ran along the shore of Chilika lake. Then it went north, through Cuttack and Jajpur, toward Bhadrak. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

Carts on the road carried goods too. They carried paddy, a kind of unhusked rice. They carried salt as well. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.147; Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

## For scholars

The road was only finished in 1825. So nothing earlier is claimed for this line. By the early 1900s, colonial records say its main goods traffic was small. It was mostly paddy and salt, over one short stretch near Balasore. That was much less than the pilgrim crowds who walked it. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.147; Patnaik, 2014, p.107]
