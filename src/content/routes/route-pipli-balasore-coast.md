Pipli was once the busiest port on the Orissa coast. Around 1514, Portuguese traders built a colony there, at what old records call "a fine harbour." [Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, pp. 37-40]

Sand then blocked the mouth of the river at Pipli. By the early 1700s, the silting had ruined the port. Traders moved their business down the coast to Balasore instead, which then became "the only port of which Orissa could boast." [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley, Bengal District Gazetteers: Balasore (1907), pp. 9, 204]

## For scholars

Both sources describe a shift of trade from Pipli to Balasore, as a river called the Subarnarekha silted up. Neither describes ships sailing this exact short hop, so the line drawn is schematic. The two sources give different dates for when the Portuguese arrived at Pipli. One source says about 1514; the other says 1599. They also disagree on whether the English ever ran a trading post there at all. [Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, pp. 37-40; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley, Bengal District Gazetteers: Balasore (1907), pp. 9, 204]
