Boats went far up the Mahanadi river, from the coast to Sambalpur. They carried salt, spices, coconuts and brass pots. Old records call this "the great road leading along the Mahanadi to Sembelpur." Traders once sent more than 300,000 maunds of salt this way each year. [Stirling, 1825, pp. 194, 218]

Boats came back down loaded with different goods. They carried cotton, wheat, oil-seeds, ghee, iron, turmeric, silk cloth and rice. [Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, Appendix III, p. 74]

Diamonds worked near Sambalpur, at Hirakud, moved along this route too. Later, salt began arriving by rail instead of by river boat. This shows the river carried it first. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., vol. XXII, pp. 12-13 (Sambalpur District)]

## For scholars

Sambalpur sits roughly 300 km inland. This river route never reached the sea. Cuttack stands in here as the nearest major river hub. All three sources describe real two-way traffic, goods moving both up and down river, not just two places that happen to exist. That places this route above some of the inferred land routes in this atlas. None of the three sources gives a boat type, a date, or a mapped course. The line shown is schematic. [Stirling, 1825, pp. 194, 218; Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, Appendix III, p. 74; Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., vol. XXII, pp. 12-13 (Sambalpur District)]
