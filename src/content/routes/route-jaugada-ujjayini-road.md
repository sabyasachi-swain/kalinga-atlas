Ashoka's rule from Dhauli was carved again at Jaugada. It said an officer had to travel from Ujjayini, a city far to the west, at least every three years. Jaugada and Dhauli were the two towns that ran Ashoka's new province of Kalinga. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause AA, p. 97 (Jaugada copy pp. 111-116); identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]

Scholars agree Ujjayini and Kalinga were both run by the same Mauryan government. A prince governed in the west, at Ujjain, and another in Kalinga, in the east. [Kulke, 1998, 3rd edn (Routledge, 1998), p. 67]

## For scholars

This edict orders a real travel schedule, just as at Dhauli. But no source maps the road itself. So the line shown here is schematic. The Jaugada copy of this rule is addressed to officials at Samapa, not Tosali. So this route is recorded on its own, apart from the Dhauli-to-Ujjayini route. Both rest on the same edict clause, though. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum I (1925), First Separate Rock Edict, clause AA, p. 97 (Jaugada copy pp. 111-116); identification pp. xxxvii-xxxviii]
