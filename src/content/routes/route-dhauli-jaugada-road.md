Ashoka had his officers carve the same orders on rocks at Dhauli and at Jaugada. One copy speaks to officials at Tosali, near Dhauli. The other speaks to officials at Samapa, near Jaugada. Historians think the two towns ran Ashoka's new province of Kalinga together. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum I (1925), pp. xiii-xv, xxxvii-xxxviii]

Scholars describe the edicts at both places as one Mauryan government. Its governor sat at Tosali. [Kulke, 1998, 3rd edn (Routledge, 1998), pp. 63-64]

## For scholars

No source here describes an actual road between Dhauli and Jaugada. It does not describe a real trip either. The link drawn is a guess. Both towns carried the same imperial orders. Both belonged to one province, run from Tosali. The distance, about 100 km, is schematic. The line shown is read off a modern map. It is not a surveyed route. [Hultzsch, 1925, Hultzsch, Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum I (1925), pp. xiii-xv, xxxvii-xxxviii; Kulke, 1998, 3rd edn (Routledge, 1998), pp. 63-64]
