Sisupalgarh was a huge walled city, a little way inland. Manikapatna was a port on Chilika lake, not far away. Diggers found the very same kind of pottery at both places. It is called rouletted ware, a fine pottery with a dotted pattern. A third site, Radhanagara, had it too. Scholars read this shared pottery as a sign of one trade network across the Bay of Bengal. [Patra, n.d., p. 110]

Historians think inland cities sent their goods to the nearest port first, and then on by sea to bigger ports. This is the general pattern one source describes for old Odisha. [Patra, 2014, p. 118]

## For scholars

No source read here records an actual journey or road between Sisupalgarh and Manikapatna. The link is inferred, from the shared pottery style and from a general pattern of inland cities using their nearest port. Sisupalgarh sits roughly 70 km inland of Chilika lake, by the shortest line. No site along the way was checked for a link. The line shown here is schematic. [Patra, n.d., p. 110; Patra, 2014, p. 118]
