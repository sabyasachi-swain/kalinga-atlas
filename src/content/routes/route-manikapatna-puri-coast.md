An old English sailing book gave a course for this coast. It was written in 1679. It told captains to steer east-north-east from Manikapatna. That course led to the great temple of Jagannath, at Puri. One historian reports this. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

Ship logs from the 1600s and 1680s also named the temple. They used it as a landmark. This probably helped sailors find the coast from far out at sea. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

Puri itself made a poor harbour. A colonial report calls it "nothing but an unprotected roadstead." The sea there runs deep, close to shore. But heavy waves kept ships from landing safely for much of the year. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.15; Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

## For scholars

Only one historian reports this sailing direction. The old book's spelling, "Maneclapatam," is his own reading of the name. The original page has not been seen here. His distance of ten miles is far short of the true gap between the two places. So either the name or the distance may be off. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]
