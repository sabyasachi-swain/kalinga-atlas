Arikamedu sits near modern Puducherry. It was a well-known trading post. It dealt with the Roman world two thousand years ago. Historians think Odisha's coast was linked into this same trade network. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Patnaik, 2014, p.103]

The same stamped pottery has been found at three places. It turns up at Manikapatna, at Sisupalgarh, and at Arikamedu. It is called rouletted ware. A historian groups this pottery with finds from Kaveripattinam and Uraiyur. Together they form one shared trade network from Roman times. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Patnaik, 2014, p.103]

No record of an actual voyage between these two coasts has been found. Historians think ships probably worked the coast in short hops. They likely joined this wider trading world piece by piece, not in one long trip. [Patnaik, 2014, p.103]

## For scholars

This tie rests only on matching pots. It is not backed by a written record of any voyage. Broken storage jars called amphorae once held wine or oil from the west. They turn up at Manikapatna and Palur, as part of this same network. But no source read for this route puts them on this exact leg. Kaveripattinam belongs to the same network too. It would suit this line just as well as Arikamedu does. [Dayalan, 2019, p.12; Patra, n.d., p.110]
