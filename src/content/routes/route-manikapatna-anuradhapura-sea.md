Ships sailed from the Chilika coast across the Bay of Bengal. Their goal was Sri Lanka. A Chinese monk named Xuanzang wrote about this coast. He said that on a clear night, sailors could see a light. It shone over a shrine on the island. [Xuanzang, 7th century CE, p.205; Patra, 2014, p.120]

A coin of a Sri Lankan king was found at Manikapatna. The king's name was Sahassamalla. His coins also turned up in Sri Lanka. They turned up on the island of Sumatra too. Historians agree this shows a trade network. It joined the Chilika coast, Sri Lanka and Sumatra. [Patra, 2014, p.120]

The same stamped pottery has been found at Anuradhapura too. It is called rouletted ware. Pottery like it turns up in Bali and at Arikamedu as well. This places Sri Lanka's old capital in the same wide trade world as Kalinga. [Ardika, 1991, p.225]

An old Sri Lankan story tells of a prince and a princess. They sailed from the Kalinga coast. They carried a tooth said to be the Buddha's. It went all the way to Sri Lanka. [Patra, n.d., p.114]

## For scholars

Anuradhapura is inland, not a harbour. Ships would have landed at a coast port, such as Mantai. No source read here ties Mantai to Kalinga directly. Xuanzang says a light was seen from Sri Lanka. He does not describe an actual sailing. The tooth-relic story comes from an old book called the Dathavamsa. It is a tradition, not a dig. Sahassamalla's coin dates to about 1200 CE. That is roughly 1,100 years after the rouletted pottery. [Xuanzang, 7th century CE, p.206; Patra, n.d., p.112]
