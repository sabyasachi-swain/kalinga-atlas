Cuttack sits inland. It stands where the Mahanadi river splits into two arms. A canal carried steamers from Cuttack down to Chandbali, a river port near the coast. Steamers ran this route three times a week. Then they carried on to Calcutta by sea. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.92; Stirling, 1825, p.194]

A colonial report called Chandbali the most important port in all of Orissa. It stood about 20 miles up the Baitarani river from the sea. Steamers on this canal carried salt and rice. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.10; Stirling, 1825, p.194]

Long before steamers came, boats had carried salt and grain along the Mahanadi. This trade went on for a very long time. In the 1820s, a British officer wrote about a huge yearly trade in salt. It moved along an old road, next to the river, toward Sambalpur. That was a different, older road, not this canal. But it shows how long the Mahanadi has carried goods. [Stirling, 1825, p.194]

## For scholars

The line on the map for this canal is only a rough guess. No source read for this route maps the canal itself. Cuttack lies far from the open sea. Big ocean-going ships never sailed this far inland. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.92; Stirling, 1825, p.194]
