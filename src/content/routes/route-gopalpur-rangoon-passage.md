Gopalpur-on-Sea had no harbour at all. Ships had to anchor out in open water. Colonial officials called it "an open roadstead with no shelter whatever." [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.329]

From this open beach, steamers probably crossed the Bay of Bengal. They sailed to Rangoon, in Burma. A colonial record gives numbers for one year. In that year, 7,400 people sailed from Gopalpur to Burma. And 8,300 came home again. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.329]

This crossing was mostly a trip for workers, not goods. No record here lists any cargo on this leg. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.152]

## For scholars

Both facts here come from the same Imperial Gazetteer. So they count as one source, not two. That keeps this route at Probable. A different gazetteer names Arakan, not Rangoon, as a trading partner for Balasore. It only shows the wider shipping world this route sat inside. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.152; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.144]
