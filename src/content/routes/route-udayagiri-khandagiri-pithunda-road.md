King Kharavela carved his own record of his rule into a rock cave. It says he destroyed a market town called Pithunda. He used a "plough of asses" to do it, in his eleventh year as king. His army marched out from near his capital, by the Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves. [Jayaswal, 1930, Jayaswal & Banerji, Epigraphia Indica XX (1929-30), line 11, translation p. 88]

Historians think Kharavela's army moved down the coast to reach Pithunda. Scholars point to Ashoka's edicts at Jaugada, far south of the actual site of his war at Dhauli, as a sign of this same southern route. [Patra, n.d., p. 113]

Pithunda was probably near the Chicacole region of modern Andhra Pradesh, south of Palur on the same coast. [Patra, 2014, p. 123]

## For scholars

Kharavela's inscription records that Pithunda was destroyed. It does not describe the route his army took to get there. No one has excavated or located Pithunda on the ground. The endpoint used here is a rough regional marker, not a dig site. Two of the three sources share the same author. This line is a schematic coastal corridor. It is not a proven march route. [Jayaswal, 1930, Jayaswal & Banerji, Epigraphia Indica XX (1929-30), line 11, translation p. 88; Patra, n.d., p. 113; Patra, 2014, p. 123]
