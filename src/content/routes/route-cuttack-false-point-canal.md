False Point was the spot where the Mahanadi river met the open sea. Old records name Cuttack city, False Point port and Chandbali together as the region's chief trade centres. Cuttack city was "connected by canal with Chandbali and False Point." [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., Imperial Gazetteer of India, vol. XI, pp. 92, 98]

A separate record calls False Point "the only anchorage on the coast." It also describes it as exposed and silting up. This was the sea end of the same canal system named above. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Cousins, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Cuttack, 2nd edn (1933), pp. 7-8]

## For scholars

Neither record maps the canal itself. The line drawn here is schematic, following the Mahanadi's branching channels on a modern map. No cargo specific to this route was found in the two sources read. False Point itself was a silting, open anchorage, not a sheltered harbour. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., Imperial Gazetteer of India, vol. XI, pp. 92, 98; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Cousins, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Cuttack, 2nd edn (1933), pp. 7-8]
