Jaugada was a walled town on the Rushikulya river, in south Odisha. The ancient emperor Ashoka had thirteen of his orders carved into a rock here. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.72]

A historian thinks Jaugada's edicts stand here because of a port nearby. That port was Palur, down near the mouth of the same river. Palur was known well beyond Kalinga. [Patra, n.d., p.113]

One scholar traced the old name Palura back to words meaning "tooth city." He guessed this pointed to elephant tusks. Tusks were probably an important export from here. Kongoda, the land around Chilika, was known for breeding good elephants. [Patra, 2014, p.122]

## For scholars

No source read here truly describes a road between Jaugada and Palur. The link is one historian's own idea, based on where the edicts happen to sit. The "tooth city" and ivory idea is also just one scholar's guess about the name. It is not a proven fact. [Patra, n.d., p.113; Patra, 2014, p.122]
