Sembiran is a village on the north coast of Bali, in Indonesia. Diggers worked there in 1987 and 1989. They found buried Indian pots, about two thousand years old. [Ardika, 1991, p.221]

The pots included rouletted ware. That is a fine stamped ware, also found at Kalinga's ports. Diggers also found a piece scratched with letters. The letters are in an old Indian script called Kharoshthi. [Ardika, 1991, p.225]

Historians think these finds show ships from India reached Bali long ago, about two thousand years back. Those ships most likely used the same trade routes as Kalinga's own ports. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Ardika, 1991, p.221]

No written record names a ship or its captain. So the link between Sembiran and Kalinga rests only on pots that look alike. [Ardika, 1991, p.225]

## For scholars

The team who dug at Sembiran never wrote about Kalinga. That link comes only from a separate Odisha journal, based on matching pots. A scratched Kharoshthi letter at Sembiran is odd next to one found at Manikapatna. But no source ties the two together, so that idea is not made here. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Ardika, 1991, p.225]
