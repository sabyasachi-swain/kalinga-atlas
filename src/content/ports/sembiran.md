Sembiran is a village on the north coast of Bali, in Indonesia. Archaeologists digging there in 1987 and 1989 found buried Indian pottery, about two thousand years old. [Ardika, 1991, p.221]

The pottery included rouletted ware, a fine stamped ware also found at Kalinga's ports, and a piece scratched with letters in an old Indian script called Kharoshthi. [Ardika, 1991, p.225]

Historians think these finds show that ships from India, most likely sailing the same network used by Kalinga's own ports, reached Bali about two thousand years ago. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Ardika, 1991, p.221]

No written record names a ship or a captain, so the link between Sembiran and Kalinga rests only on pottery that looks alike. [Ardika, 1991, p.225]

## For scholars

The scholars who dug at Sembiran never mention Kalinga; that link is made only by a separate Odisha journal, based on matching pottery styles. A scratched Kharoshthi letter at Sembiran is intriguing next to one found at Manikapatna, but no source connects the two directly, so that idea is not claimed here. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Ardika, 1991, p.225]
