Pipli once stood near the mouth of the Subarnarekha river, in north Odisha. Long ago, it had one of the finest harbours on the coast. [Hunter, 1872, p.37]

Portuguese traders settled here and built a busy port. Old records call it a great slave mart, a place where enslaved people were bought and sold. Dutch traders came here too, after the Portuguese. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.204; Hunter, 1872, p.37]

Slowly, sand built up and blocked the river mouth. By the early 1700s, ships could no longer reach Pipli. The busy port was left empty. [Hunter, 1872, p.40; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.9]

Today, nothing of the old town is left. A British report from 1907 called Pipli "the most important port on the Orissa coast." But its exact spot has vanished under shifting sand. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., pp.9, 204]

## For scholars

Sources do not agree when the Portuguese first came, or if the English ever built a post here. Hunter says yes. Another old British record quotes a historian's doubt: the English "never had any factory at Pipli except in the imagination of the historians." [Hunter, 1872, p.37; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.204]
