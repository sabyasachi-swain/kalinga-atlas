Pipli once stood near the mouth of the Subarnarekha river, in north Odisha. Long ago, it had one of the finest harbours on the coast. [Hunter, 1872, p.37]

Portuguese traders settled here and built a busy port. Old records call it a great market town. Dutch traders came here too, after the Portuguese. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.204; Hunter, 1872, p.37]

Slowly, sand built up and blocked the river mouth. By the early 1700s, ships could no longer reach Pipli. The busy port was left empty. [Hunter, 1872, p.40]

Today, nothing of the old town is left. Historians agree it was once the busiest port on this coast. But its exact spot has vanished under shifting sand. [Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

## For scholars

Sources do not agree when the Portuguese first came, or if the English ever built a post here. Hunter says yes. Another old British record says the English post "existed only in the imagination of the historians." [Hunter, 1872, p.37; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.204]
