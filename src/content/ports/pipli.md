Pipli once stood near the mouth of the Subarnarekha river, in north Odisha. Long ago, it had one of the finest harbours on the coast. [Hunter, 1872, p.37]

Portuguese traders settled here and built a busy port. Records call it a great marketplace, where Dutch traders also came after them. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.204; Hunter, 1872, p.37]

Slowly, sand and silt built up and blocked the river mouth. By the early 1700s, ships could no longer reach Pipli, and the busy port was abandoned. [Hunter, 1872, p.40]

Today, nothing of the old town is left. Historians agree it was once the busiest port on this stretch of coast, but its exact site has vanished under the delta's shifting sand. [Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

## For scholars

Sources do not agree when the Portuguese arrived, or whether the English ever built a factory here. Hunter says yes; another British record says the English factory "existed only in the imagination of the historians." [Hunter, 1872, p.37; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.204]
