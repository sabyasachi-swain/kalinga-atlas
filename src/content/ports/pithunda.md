An old inscription names a town called Pithunda, on the Kalinga coast, more than two thousand years ago. It calls Pithunda a market-town, though even the scholars who translated the inscription were unsure of that word. The inscription for King Kharavela says he destroyed Pithunda in war. It says an earlier king first founded the town. That king is called the Ava king, though where he ruled is not known. [Patra, 2014, p.123; Jayaswal, 1930, p.88]

No one has ever dug up Pithunda. So no one is sure exactly where it stood. One idea is that it lay near modern Srikakulam. That is close to the coast of north Andhra Pradesh. [Dayalan, 2019, p.21]

One idea links Pithunda to a city a Greek mapmaker named Ptolemy called Pityndra. It may also be the town Pihumda, named in an old Jain text. If that idea is right, one market town was known by three different names. [Patra, 2014, p.123]

## For scholars

The dot on the map marks only the Srikakulam region. It is not a real dug-up site. Even the writer Dayalan gives two different guesses for Pithunda in one paper. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20]
