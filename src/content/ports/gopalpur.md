Gopalpur-on-Sea was the main port of the Ganjam coast, in south Odisha. Ships anchored right out in the open sea, since there is no bay or shelter here at all. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.329]

In British times, steamers stopped here often. Thousands of workers sailed from Gopalpur to Burma each year looking for jobs, and returned the same way. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.329]

Some scholars think Gopalpur may stand near a much older port, the place from which Ptolemy, a Greek geographer, said ships left the coast to sail toward "Khryse," a legendary land of gold. This idea is not settled. [Dayalan, 2019, p.21]

Other historians prefer a different spot nearby for that old port, so Gopalpur's own clear story is best told from British times onward. [Patra, 2014, p.121]

## For scholars

Only the colonial-era port at Gopalpur is well documented. Its link to Ptolemy's ancient port is a guess by one early scholar, and even Patra, who reports it, prefers a different nearby site. [Patra, 2014, p.121; Dayalan, 2019, p.21]
