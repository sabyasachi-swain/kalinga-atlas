Gopalpur-on-Sea was the main port of the Ganjam coast, in south Odisha. Ships had to anchor right out in the open sea. There is no bay here to hide from the waves. [The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.329]

In British times, steamers stopped here often. In the year 1903-4 alone, more than seven thousand passengers sailed from Gopalpur to Burma, and over eight thousand came back the same way. [The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.329]

Some scholars think Gopalpur sits near a much older port. Long ago, a Greek mapmaker named Ptolemy wrote of a spot where ships left the coast to sail toward "Khryse," a storied land of gold. This idea is not settled. [Dayalan, 2019, p.21]

Other historians pick a different nearby spot for that old port. So Gopalpur's own clear story is best told from British times on. [Patra, 2014, p.121]

## For scholars

Only the British-era port at Gopalpur is well recorded. Its tie to Ptolemy's old port is just one scholar's guess. Even Patra, who reports it, picks a different nearby spot instead. [Patra, 2014, p.121; Dayalan, 2019, p.21]
