Chandbali is a river port far up the Baitarani river, about 20 miles from the sea. Being so far inland kept its ships safe from storms out at sea. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.197; Hunter, 1872, p.107]

The channel here was declared an official port in 1858. Before that, it grew from the trade of an older port nearby, called Dhamra. [Hunter, 1872, p.107]

A hundred years ago, steamers left Chandbali for Calcutta three times a week, carrying passengers and goods along a canal from Cuttack. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.92; Hunter, 1872, p.107]

Historians agree Chandbali traded with Calcutta, Bombay, Arakan, the Maldives and Ceylon, making it one of Odisha's two most important ports in British times, alongside Balasore. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.144; Hunter, 1872, p.107]

## For scholars

Chandbali is a young port. Records say it was only a few fishing huts until about 1872, so it should not be counted among the older 1500s-1700s trading ports of this coast. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.197; Hunter, 1872, p.107]
