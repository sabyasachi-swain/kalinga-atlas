Kaveripattinam was a great port where the Kaveri river meets the sea, in Tamil Nadu. It is also called Kaveripumpattinam, or Puhar, in old texts. [Dayalan, 2019, p.10]

Diggers found a huge brick and timber platform there, built as a wharf where boats could tie up. Wood from the site dates to about 250 BCE, showing the port is very old. [Dayalan, 2019, p.11]

The same rouletted ware, a fine stamped pottery, found here also turns up at Kalinga's ports. Historians think an overland trade road once linked this southern port to Kalinga's own centres, alongside a shared sea trade network. [Patnaik, 2014, p.103; Patra, n.d., p.110]

No source describes a direct sea voyage between the two coasts, so historians call this link probable, not proven. [Dayalan, 2019, p.10]

## For scholars

As with Arikamedu, Kaveripattinam's tie to Kalinga rests on shared pottery and an old trade road, not a recorded voyage. The excavation report behind the wharf description has not been checked directly. [Dayalan, 2019, p.11]
