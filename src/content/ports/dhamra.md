Dhamra is a port where two big rivers, the Brahmani and the Baitarani, join and flow out to sea. A government order made it an official port in 1858. Old records rank it right after Balasore among Odisha's harbours. [Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, Appendix IV, pp. 107-108]

A sandbank at the river mouth made the entrance tricky. Even so, ships as deep as 18 feet could pass through safely. Later, a nearby port called Chandbali took over most of Dhamra's trade. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley, Bengal District Gazetteers: Balasore (1907), p. 8]

## For scholars

No source used here describes Dhamra before the 1858 order. So only the British period is tagged for this entry. River trade at this river mouth before that time is possible, but unverified. Neither record gives a printed coordinate for the port. Its position is read off a modern map, at the Dhamra river mouth in Bhadrak district. [Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, Appendix IV, pp. 107-108; District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley, Bengal District Gazetteers: Balasore (1907), p. 8]
