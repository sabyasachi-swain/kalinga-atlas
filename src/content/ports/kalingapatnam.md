Kalingapatnam sits where the Vamsadhara river flows into the sea, in what is now Andhra Pradesh. Its name means "the town of the Kalinga people," and ships used this coast for well over two thousand years. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20]

Archaeologists digging here in 1928, and again in the 1970s, found thick brick walls, glazed tiles and old pottery. The finds point to a walled town that stood from about the 200s BCE into medieval times. Roman coins turned up in the same soil, a sign of trade reaching far across the sea. [Patra, 2014, p.122]

Kalingapatnam was also a Buddhist centre, with monks living and teaching close to the working harbour. Historians agree that under the Eastern Ganga kings, ships sailed from here to Sri Lanka, Java, Sumatra and other lands across the sea. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20]

## For scholars

Kalingapatnam lies in modern Andhra Pradesh, on the old Kalinga coast, not in today's Odisha. British records also describe ruined mosques and old gold coins found on the site, and a lighthouse that once guided ships to the river mouth. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.291; Dayalan, 2019, p.20]
