Maliwan is a very old port town. It sits in southern Myanmar. It is on gentle hills, next to a river. The river flows into the Bay of Bengal. Archaeologists dated the site. They used two radiocarbon tests. It was used from the fourth to the second century BCE. [Bellina, 2018, Antiquity 92(366), e6 (2018), pp. 3, 5]

Diggers worked at Maliwan and a nearby site, Aw Gyi. They found many objects made in India. These include beads made of carnelian and glass. They also found fine grey pottery and clay figures in an Indian style. [Dayalan, 2019, offprint p. 23]

## For scholars

Maliwan is not a Kalinga port. It is a partner site in the wider Bay of Bengal trade network. The 2018 report covers only the first digging season. Its dates rest on just two radiocarbon tests. Its date range starts earlier than this atlas's Mauryan period. So the period tag here is only a rough fit. No source prints an exact coordinate. The position given is the modern village of Maliwan. It is read from a map, not the actual dig site. [Bellina, 2018, Antiquity 92(366), e6 (2018), pp. 3, 5; Dayalan, 2019, offprint p. 23]
