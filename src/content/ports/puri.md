Puri is the town of the great Jagannath Temple, right beside the Bay of Bengal. Its tall temple towers could be seen far out at sea, so sailors probably steered toward it to find the coast. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

Old English ship logs from the 1600s and 1680s mention sailing to Puri. A historian who studied them thinks Puri worked as a landing place from as early as the 900s CE. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

But Puri's beach makes a poor harbour. Colonial records call it an unprotected roadstead, a stretch of open shore with no shelter from the waves, that ships could use safely only for half the year. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.15; Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

So historians think ships probably anchored offshore and used small boats to reach the beach, rather than sailing right up to a dock. [Patnaik, 2014, p.107]

## For scholars

Sources disagree on Puri. British gazetteers call it a place with almost no trade at all. Patnaik argues from ship logs that boats used it for centuries. Both things can be true: a beach that ships could reach, but never a real harbour. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.15; Patnaik, 2014, p.107]
