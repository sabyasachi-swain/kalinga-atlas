Sambalpur is a town far up the Mahanadi river, deep inland. River boats carried salt, spices, coconuts and brass pots up to the town. They came back loaded with cotton, wheat, oil-seeds, ghee, iron, turmeric, silk cloth and rice. [Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, Appendix III, p. 74]

Salt used to be a big export from Sambalpur. Traders once sent more than 300,000 maunds of salt out each year, along the road by the Mahanadi. Money from the salt trade was worth more than all the land rents put together. [Stirling, 1825, pp. 194, 218]

Old records say diamonds were once worked at Hirakud, near Sambalpur, under local rulers. The area had 160 native iron furnaces. Its sal forest covered 238 square miles, and tasar silk weaving was an important trade there too. Later, salt reached Sambalpur by rail instead of by river boat. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., vol. XXII, pp. 12-13 (Sambalpur District)]

## For scholars

Sambalpur is a river and land trade point, about 300 km inland. It was never a sea port. The diamond-working is dated only to "native rule", the pre-British Chauhan state of Sambalpur. The furnace, forest and rail facts come from British-period records. No source gives a printed coordinate for the town; its position is read off a modern map. [Stirling, 1825, pp. 194, 218; Hunter, 1872, vol. ii, Appendix III, p. 74; Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., vol. XXII, pp. 12-13 (Sambalpur District)]
