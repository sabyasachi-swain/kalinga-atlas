Tamralipti was the greatest port at the top of the Bay of Bengal, where the Rupnarayan river meets the sea. Today the spot is called Tamluk, in West Bengal. [Patra, 2014, p.118]

Buddhist monks and traders sailed from here to Sri Lanka, Burma and China. Three famous Chinese pilgrims, Fa-Hien, Xuanzang and Yijing, all left from Tamralipti on their journeys home. [Patra, 2014, p.119]

Diggers found huge amounts of rouletted ware there, a fine pottery stamped with tiny rolled patterns, the same kind found at Kalinga's own ports. An old inscription also records merchants from Ayodhya visiting the port. [Dayalan, 2019, p.24]

Historians agree Tamralipti linked Kalinga's coast to the wider world. A coin of the Eastern Ganga kings, who also ruled Kalinga, was found at Tamralipti, hinting that the two coasts traded closely together. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Patra, 2014, p.119]

## For scholars

Some Odisha writers claim Tamralipti was once part of a Kalinga kingdom. That claim is disputed and is not repeated here; Tamralipti is included only as a trading partner just outside Kalinga. [Patra, 2014, p.119; Dayalan, 2019, p.24]
