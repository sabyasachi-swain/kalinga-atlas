One historian calls Tamralipti "the first and foremost" port at the top of the Bay of Bengal. It stood where the Rupnarayan river meets the sea. Today the spot is called Tamluk, in West Bengal. [Patra, 2014, pp.118-119]

Buddhist monks and traders sailed from here to Sri Lanka, Burma and China. Three famous Chinese monks left from Tamralipti on their way home. Their names were Fa-Hien, Xuanzang and Yijing. [Patra, 2014, p.119]

Diggers found huge amounts of rouletted ware there. That is a fine pottery stamped with tiny rolled lines. The same kind turns up at Kalinga's own ports. An old inscription also names merchants from Ayodhya who visited the port. [Dayalan, 2019, p.24; Patra, n.d., p.110]

Historians agree Tamralipti linked Kalinga's coast to the wider world. A coin of the Eastern Ganga kings turned up at Tamralipti too. Those same kings ruled Kalinga. This hints the two coasts traded closely together. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Patra, 2014, p.119]

## For scholars

Some Odisha writers say Tamralipti was once part of a Kalinga kingdom. That claim is disputed, so it is not repeated here. Tamralipti is included only as a trading partner just outside Kalinga. [Patra, 2014, p.119; Dayalan, 2019, p.24]
