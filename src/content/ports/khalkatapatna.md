Khalkatapatna was a busy river port near Konark, on the coast of Odisha. It stood on the bank of the Kushabhadra river, not far from the sea. Archaeologists dug there in the 1980s and found a hard brick floor, built for loading and unloading boats. [Patra, 2014, p.119]

Diggers also turned up piles of Chinese pottery, including blue-patterned porcelain and a shiny green ware called celadon. Two round Chinese copper coins, each with a square hole in the middle, showed the port was trading with China around the 1300s CE. [Patnaik, 2014, p.105]

Diggers found more than a hundred small objects nearby, including beads, bangles and coins made by local Odisha kings. Together, the finds show Khalkatapatna was a working harbour, not just a fishing village. [Patra, n.d., p.112; Patnaik, 2014, p.105]

Historians agree Khalkatapatna flourished under the Eastern Ganga kings, the same rulers who built the great Sun Temple at nearby Konark. [Dayalan, 2019, p.22]

## For scholars

Sources do not agree on the excavation year. Two say 1984-85, but one says 1987. No official excavation report has been checked to settle this. [Patra, 2014, p.119; Patnaik, 2014, p.105]
