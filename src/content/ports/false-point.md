False Point is a spot on the Odisha coast where the Mahanadi river meets the sea. Old records call it "the only anchorage on the coast" for ships in this stretch. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Cousins, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Cuttack, 2nd edn (1933), pp. 7-8]

Ships could not shelter here. It was an open anchorage, exposed to the sea, and it was filling up with sand fast. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Cousins, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Cuttack, 2nd edn (1933), pp. 7-8]

Other records name False Point as one of the region's main trade centres, alongside Cuttack city and Chandbali. A canal connected Cuttack to both Chandbali and False Point. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., Imperial Gazetteer of India, vol. XI, pp. 92, 98]

## For scholars

Both records describe an exposed, silting anchorage, not a sheltered harbour. Neither gives an opening date for the port. Some popular accounts say it opened in 1860, but that wording was not found in the two records read here. No printed coordinate was found; the position given is read off a modern map, at the False Point mouth in Kendrapara district. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., O'Malley & Cousins, Bihar and Orissa District Gazetteers: Cuttack, 2nd edn (1933), pp. 7-8]
