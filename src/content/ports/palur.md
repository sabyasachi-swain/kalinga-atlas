Palur is a small village at the mouth of the Rushikulya river, in Ganjam district, south Odisha. Long ago it stood on a busy stretch of coast. An old inscription found at Nagarjunikonda, carved in the late 200s CE, names Palur as a trading port. [Patra, 2014, p.121]

Archaeologists exploring a spot near Palur called Kantigarh found broken Chinese pottery. They also found rouletted ware, a fine pottery stamped with tiny rolled patterns, and pieces of storage jars called amphorae, which once carried wine or oil from lands around the Mediterranean Sea. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20]

Some scholars think Palur is the same place the Greek geographer Ptolemy called Paloura, almost two thousand years ago. Other scholars link an old name, Dantapura, to this same coast. Neither idea is settled, but together they hint at how far Palur's name once travelled. [Patra, 2014, p.121]

## For scholars

No excavation report has been read for the Kantigarh finds. They come only from short survey notes, so the pottery is not yet dated closely, and it has not been directly linked to any shipwreck or voyage. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20; Patra, n.d., p.109]
