Palur is a small village where the Rushikulya river meets the sea. It sits in Ganjam district, south Odisha. Long ago it stood on a busy coast. An old inscription found at Nagarjunikonda names Palur as a trading port. It was carved in the late 200s CE. [Patra, 2014, p.121]

Diggers explored a nearby spot called Kantigarh. They found broken Chinese pots there. They also found rouletted ware, a fine pottery stamped with tiny rolled lines. And they found pieces of storage jars called amphorae. These jars once held wine or oil from lands far to the west. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20]

Some scholars think Palur is the same place a Greek mapmaker named Ptolemy called Paloura. That was almost two thousand years ago. The idea is not proven. But it hints at how far Palur's name once travelled. [Patra, 2014, p.121]

A newspaper report on a 2023-25 dig at Palur describes nine layers of settlement, almost three metres deep, running from before the Mauryan period through the Early Historic and Early Medieval periods. Diggers found northern black polished ware, knobbed ware, and 363 pottery pieces scratched with graffiti marks. The full excavation report is not out yet: this account comes from a newspaper story about a conference talk, so it is one early report, not a finished dig report. [Sahu, 2026, Times of India, 1 August 2026]

## For scholars

No dig report has been read for the Kantigarh finds. They come only from short survey notes. So the pottery is not dated closely yet. It has not been linked to any one ship or voyage either. [Dayalan, 2019, p.20; Patra, n.d., p.109]
