Cuttack stands where the Mahanadi river splits into two arms. It sits in the middle of Odisha. Its name, Kataka, means "the fort." Boats used the river and a canal to reach the coast ports of Chandbali and False Point. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.98; Hunter, 1872, p.40]

In the 1600s, English traders bought fine cotton cloth from Cuttack's weavers. This cloth was called muslin. They paid very low prices for it. Then they shipped it down the river to their posts on the coast. [Hunter, 1872, p.40]

Diggers at Cuttack's old Barabati fort found a few pieces of Chinese pottery. The fort sits beside the Mahanadi river. This small find hints that goods from far away reached this inland town too. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Hunter, 1872, p.40]

Historians agree Cuttack was a river port, not a sea port. Big ships never reached this far inland. But boats and canals carried goods between Cuttack and the coast for hundreds of years. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.98; Hunter, 1872, p.40]

## For scholars

An old story says a king built Cuttack's fort in the 900s CE. But no proof backs this up. The Chinese pottery from Barabati fort is known only second-hand. No one has checked the first dig report yet. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Hunter, 1872, p.40]
