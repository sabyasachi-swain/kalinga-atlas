Cuttack stands on a strip of land where the Mahanadi river splits into two branches, in the middle of Odisha. Its name, Kataka, means "the fort." Boats used the river and a canal to reach the coastal ports of Chandbali and False Point. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.98; Hunter, 1872, p.40]

In the 1600s, English merchants bought fine cotton cloth called muslin from Cuttack's weavers, at very low prices. They shipped this cloth down the river to their trading posts on the coast. [Hunter, 1872, p.40]

Diggers at Cuttack's old Barabati fort, beside the Mahanadi, found a few pieces of Chinese pottery. This small find hints that goods from far away reached this inland town too. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Hunter, 1872, p.40]

Historians agree Cuttack worked as a river port, not a sea port. Ocean-going ships never reached this far inland, but boats and canals carried goods between Cuttack and the coast for hundreds of years. [Imperial Gazetteer of India (Orissa entries), n.d., p.98; Hunter, 1872, p.40]

## For scholars

A local tradition says a Somavamshi-era king built Cuttack's fort in the 900s CE, but no evidence proves this. The Chinese pottery from Barabati fort is reported only second-hand; the original dig report has not been checked. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Hunter, 1872, p.40]
