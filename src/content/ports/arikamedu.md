Arikamedu is an old trading village on the Ariyankuppam river, close to modern Puducherry in south India. Long ago, it may have been the port called "Poduke" in an old Greek sailing guide. [Dayalan, 2019, p.12]

Archaeologists found the same shiny, stamped pottery here, called rouletted ware, as at Kalinga's ports of Manikapatna and Sisupalgarh. Historians think this pottery links Arikamedu and Kalinga to one shared trading network, active around two thousand years ago. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Dayalan, 2019, p.12]

No record of an actual voyage between Arikamedu and Kalinga has been found. So historians are careful to call this a shared network, probably built on many short hops of trade, rather than proof of one direct sea route. [Patra, n.d., p.110; Dayalan, 2019, p.12]

## For scholars

The Kalinga link rests only on matching pottery styles, not on any written record of ships sailing between the two coasts. [Patra, n.d., p.111; Dayalan, 2019, p.12]
