Balasore sits on the Burhabalang river, near the coast of north Odisha. English traders built one of their first posts in Bengal here, in the 1630s or 1640s. They came for a stretch of water safe enough for ships to anchor. [Hunter, 1872, p.39; The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.245]

Big merchant ships had to wait far out at sea. Smaller boats then carried goods up the winding river to the town and back again. [The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.245]

For a time, Balasore was the only real port Odisha had. Ships came from Madras for rice, and traders from the Maldive and Laccadive islands brought cowrie shells, once used as money. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.144; Hunter, 1872, p.39]

Dutch, Danish and French traders also set up posts at Balasore. A colonial-era survey lists it among the Odisha ports that stayed in active trade with European merchants from the 1500s to the 1700s. [Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

## For scholars

Sources do not agree on the year Balasore's story began. Hunter says English trade started in 1642. Other British records say 1633, under a trader named Ralph Cartwright. [Hunter, 1872, p.39; The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.245]
