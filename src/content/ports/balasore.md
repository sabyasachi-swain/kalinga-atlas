Balasore sits on the Burhabalang river, near the coast of north Odisha. In 1642, English traders built their first factory in Bengal here, drawn by a stretch of water safe enough for ships to anchor. [NEEDS VERIFICATION] <!-- fact-check: the date is not settled. Hunter says 1642. He also says the first English post in Bengal was at Pipli. Other records say 1633. --> [Hunter, 1872, p.39; The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.245]

Big merchant ships had to wait far out at sea. Smaller boats then carried goods up the winding river to the town and back again. [The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.245]

For a time, Balasore was the only real port Odisha had. Ships came from Madras for rice, and traders from the Maldive and Laccadive islands brought cowrie shells, once used as money. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.144; Hunter, 1872, p.39]

Dutch, Danish and French traders also set up posts at Balasore. Historians agree it was one of the busiest ports on the whole Odisha coast for about two hundred years. [NEEDS VERIFICATION] <!-- fact-check: the page cited only lists Balasore as a trade port. It does not rank it or give a span of years. --> [Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

## For scholars

Sources do not agree on the year Balasore's story began. Hunter says English trade started in 1642. Other British records say 1633, under a trader named Ralph Cartwright. [Hunter, 1872, p.39; The Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.245]
