Manikapatna is a small village by Chilika Lake, in Puri district, Odisha. No one knew it was once a port. Then diggers worked here from 1989 to 1993. They found signs of two busy times. One began around 200 BCE. The other ran from the 800s CE to the 1800s. [Tripati, 2021, pp.1211-1212]

Diggers found broken pots from China, and from Burma and Thailand too. They found rouletted ware too. That is pottery stamped with tiny rolled lines. They also found old writing called Kharoshthi, a script from northwest India. Coins from the Puri-Kushan kings, a Chola king, and Chinese rulers turned up in the same dirt. [Patnaik, 2014, p.105; Tripati, 2021, p.1212; Patra, n.d., p.111]

One coin here came from a Sri Lankan king named Sahassamalla. The same king's coins turn up in Sri Lanka and on the island of Sumatra too. Historians agree this shows Manikapatna's ships joined trade across the whole Bay of Bengal. [Patra, 2014, p.120]

The port did not stop there. During a survey in 2018-19, diggers found a broken stone anchor near an old mosque. Its shape matches anchors used on Arab ships. This hints that traders from far west still came here in later times. [Tripati, 2022, Introduction]

## For scholars

The 1989-93 dig was run by a state institute, not the Archaeological Survey of India. No one has checked its full report page by page yet. [Tripati, 2021, p.1211] Some scholars think Manikapatna is the port Che-li-ta-lo, a spot a Chinese monk named Xuanzang wrote about. Other scholars are not sure. Two other spots have also been guessed. [Patra, 2014, p.120; Patnaik, 2014, p.104]
