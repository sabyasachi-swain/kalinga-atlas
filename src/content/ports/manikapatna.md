Manikapatna is a small village on the shore of Chilika Lake, in Puri district, Odisha. No one knew it had once been a busy port until archaeologists dug there between 1989 and 1993. Their trenches showed two long periods of trade: one starting around 200 BCE, and a second running from the 800s CE into the 1800s. [Tripati, 2021, p.1211]

Diggers found broken pottery from China, Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. They also found rouletted ware, a fine pottery stamped with tiny rolled patterns, and a scratched inscription in Kharoshthi, an old script from northwest India. Coins from the Puri-Kushan kings, the Chola king Rajaraja, and Chinese emperors turned up in the same soil. [Patnaik, 2014, p.105]

A coin of the Sri Lankan king Sahassamalla found at Manikapatna also turns up in Sri Lanka and on the island of Sumatra. Historians agree this shows Manikapatna's ships joined a trading network that stretched across the whole Bay of Bengal. [Patra, 2014, p.120]

The port kept working long after that. In 2018, explorers found a broken stone anchor near an old mosque nearby. Its shape and stone match anchors used on Arab-style ships, so it hints that traders from the Arabian Sea still called here in medieval times. [Tripati, 2022, p.13559]

## For scholars

The 1989-93 dig was run by a state research institute, not the Archaeological Survey of India, and its full report has not been checked page by page. [Tripati, 2021, p.1211] Some scholars think Manikapatna is the port Che-li-ta-lo, described by the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang. Others are not sure, and two other places have also been proposed. [Patra, 2014, p.120]
