Anuradhapura was the old capital city of Sri Lanka, set inland from the coast. It stands here for the island's link to Kalinga's ports, reached through harbours on Sri Lanka's own coast. [Patra, 2014, p.120]

A coin of the Sri Lankan king Sahassamalla, found at the old port of Polonnaruva, also turned up at Manikapatna in Kalinga and on the island of Sumatra. Historians think this shows a probable trading network linking all three places. [Patra, 2014, p.120]

Rouletted ware, a fine stamped pottery, has also been found at Anuradhapura, alongside pottery styles shared with Bali and Arikamedu. This places Sri Lanka's old capital inside the same wide Bay of Bengal trading world as Kalinga. [Ardika, 1991, p.225]

A story from an old Sri Lankan chronicle tells of a prince and princess sailing from the port of Dantapura, on the Kalinga coast, to bring a tooth relic of the Buddha to Sri Lanka. [Patra, n.d., p.114; Patra, 2014, p.120]

## For scholars

Anuradhapura itself is not a harbour; ships would have called at coastal ports such as Mantai, which no source read here directly ties to Kalinga. The tooth-relic story comes from a chronicle called the Dathavamsa, and is a tradition, not an excavation. [Patra, n.d., p.114; Patra, 2014, p.120]
