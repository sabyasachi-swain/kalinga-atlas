Hariharpur was a small trading post in the middle of the Mahanadi river delta. Ships could not reach it directly; it stood on a channel a little way inland. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.34; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

In 1633, English traders built a house of business here, hoping to reach ships passing along the delta's channels. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.35; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

Life there was hard. Fever made many traders sick, and five of the six factory workers died within a short time. The English soon gave up the post. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.35; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

Historians think Hariharpur probably worked as a small river landing for a few years, more than a busy port. [Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

## For scholars

Hariharpur may not be one place. One record calls it Hariharpur, but spells it Harishpur only two pages later, and another old list treats the two names as separate towns. No published map coordinate for the site has been found. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.35; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]
