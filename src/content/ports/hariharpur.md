Hariharpur was a small trading post in the middle of the Mahanadi river delta. It stood on a channel, at a customs post where ships could anchor offshore. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.34; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

In 1633, English traders built a house of business here. They hoped to reach ships in the delta's channels. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.35; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

Life there was hard. Fever made many of them sick. Five of the six traders died in a short time. The English soon gave up the post. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.35; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]

## For scholars

Hariharpur may not be one place. One record calls it Hariharpur. Two pages later the same record spells it Harishpur. No published map coordinate for the site has been found. [District Gazetteers (Cuttack, Puri, Ganjam, Balasore), British-era editions, n.d., p.35; Dayalan, 2019, p.23]
