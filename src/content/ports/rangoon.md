Rangoon, now called Yangon, is a busy river port in Burma. It sits about 21 miles from the sea, across open water from the Kalinga coast. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.213]

A hundred years ago, ships sailed there often from the ports of Ganjam, in south Odisha. Thousands of workers went to Rangoon each year, looking for jobs. Thousands came home the same way. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.152]

Kalinga's trade with Burma is likely much older than these British-time trips. But no source read here names an old Burmese port tied to Kalinga. So this entry tells only Rangoon's newer story. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.152]

## For scholars

Both facts about British-era Rangoon come from the same Imperial Gazetteer. So they count as one source of proof. That keeps this entry at Probable. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.213]
