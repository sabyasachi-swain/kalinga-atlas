Rangoon, now called Yangon, is a busy river port in Burma (Myanmar), about 21 miles from the sea. It stands across open water from the Kalinga coast. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.213]

A hundred years ago, ships sailed there often from the ports of Ganjam, in south Odisha. Thousands of workers travelled to Rangoon each year looking for jobs, and thousands came home again the same way. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.152]

Kalinga's trade with Burma is probably much older than these colonial-era crossings. But no source read here names an old Burmese port that worked with Kalinga, so this entry only tells Rangoon's newer, colonial story. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.152]

## For scholars

Both facts about colonial Rangoon come from the same Imperial Gazetteer, so they count as one source for evidence, keeping this entry at Probable. [Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition (Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1907-09), 1908, p.213]
